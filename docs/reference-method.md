# Turning a reference into a decision

Keep a working note with four columns: the source and snapshot, an observation made from the actual work, the new product fact it can serve, and something you chose to leave behind. One strong observation is more useful than a directory of screenshots.

## Subtraction audit

On the actual screen, temporarily hide each eyebrow, top rule, serial number, footer label, tile border and secondary title. If removing a divider makes related controls harder to locate, restore the divider. If removing a serial number costs nothing, leave it out. Apply this to effects as well. A specimen's cast shadow may explain its depth in a drawer. The same shadow around every search result rarely carries the same information.

## Color and motion

Assign jobs before choosing quantities. Inspect text on the rendered background, including over image edges and in disabled or selected states. Test whether someone can distinguish a change without color alone. Use a surprising accent where the visitor can say what it means. A palette sampled from a reference still needs to work with your own content and density.

Track what moves, the trigger, what a visitor learns and the interruption path. A first entrance may unfold. Repeated filtering should respond immediately. Deliver a stable frame for reduced-motion users and label an active or changed state in another way. On a sound-led product, try playback with the volume off and verify that on-screen progress still works. If audio quality matters, also listen on real hardware.

## Artwork assignment

Write a one-paragraph art brief with the item's identity, viewpoint, light source, material, silhouette at thumbnail size, nearest neighboring item, and two crop constraints. For a responsive layout, protect the part of the object that identifies it on a phone. Keep labels, buttons, meaningful scale markings and live data in the interface when they need to be exact.

Generate a test frame or use a rights-cleared original. Assemble it in the actual layout before commissioning variants. Export an appropriately sized local asset, note any masking or cropping, and record origin, author/credit, and terms in the asset registry. Distinguish generated illustrations of fictional objects from documentary material. Treat a screenshot of a reference as a research note unless its reuse rights have been established.

## A route family can outgrow the visual metaphor

In a collection, visitors may open a cabinet once, inspect items closely, line up two tools on a common surface, then write a note. A drawer is useful on the first screen. Requiring four drawer-opening animations to compare two objects makes the job harder. Keep the material and vocabulary where they orient the visitor. Change the layout when a different action deserves the space.
