# The last eight counts

A fictional dance collective posts rehearsal videos. Their newer dancers know most of a phrase and keep missing the transition out of count six. The useful first decision is where to begin watching.

## Direction

Test two ways into the same recording. One version opens with a large still, its full title and a conventional Play button. Another puts the names of the movements at their points on a strip of counts. Choosing “turn out” moves the film to the corresponding position. A visitor can locate the difficult transition in the second version before pressing Play. Choose that direction when the supplied rehearsal material contains reliable timing marks. With an unannotated archive, begin with an index of clips and add navigation from the information actually available.

Build the count strip with live HTML buttons. Give the selected count a full-height area of color and a written label. Crop the film so the dancer's feet remain visible at the narrowest layout. On a phone, place the sequence immediately after the video and wrap the labels. Keep timing cues available in text. The website's identity comes from working with time and spatial relationships. It needs no invented binder, candy-colored dashboard, motion background, or new illustration.

## Route choices

| Route | First decision | Composition and behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Rehearsals | Which phrase do I need? | List movement names and lengths. Put the most recent practice position near the top only when a real saved position exists. |
| Phrase | Which transition should I repeat? | Video above its count strip. A loop control selects the current phrase. Changing the start point updates the textual cue and film position immediately. |
| My practice | Which trouble spot did I mark? | Saved phrases ordered by the user's chosen sequence, with a short text note. Open straight at the marked time. |

Use one spelling for each phrase across routes. Reserve the active color for the selected point in time. A fresh visitor can start a phrase without setup. A returning dancer's bookmark opens a usable paused view at the marked transition. No entrance sequence interrupts rehearsal. Reduced-motion preference removes decorative transitions while preserving deliberate playback controls. If media is unavailable, the count, text cue and recovery link remain usable. If saving fails, leave the on-screen note in place and tell the dancer how to copy or retry it.

## What to try in the build

Open an interior link with a valid time, an out-of-range time, and an unknown phrase. Move focus across counts using the implemented keyboard controls. Select another count twice during playback. Refresh a saved practice note. Zoom until the labels wrap, then compare each label with its control. Ask a fresh reader to reach “turn out” without watching the entire clip. If they cannot, find what led them to the wrong action before adding instructions.
